Is education really free in Italy and Germany?
+
Yes — for public universities. In Germany, tuition is €0; you pay only a small semester fee (≈₹12,000–28,000). In Italy, tuition is income-based and can be as low as ₹0 — the DSU scholarship system can even give you a monthly stipend on top of the fee waiver.
Do I need to know Italian or German to study there?
+
No! Both Italy and Germany offer hundreds of programs completely in English — especially at the master's level. Some Italian bachelor's programs are also in English. For German-taught programs, you'd need German (B2/C1), but English-medium options are widely available, especially in engineering.
What IELTS band score do I need?
+
Most Italian and German universities require IELTS 6.0–6.5 for English-taught programs. Some programs may accept 5.5 or 7.0 depending on the course. We help you identify the exact requirement for your target university during counselling.
What is the blocked account for Germany?
+
Germany requires you to deposit approximately ₹9–10 lakhs into a German blocked bank account before your visa is issued. This is NOT a fee — it is your own money, released to you monthly (≈₹75K/month) for living expenses in Germany. You get every rupee back.
Can I work part-time while studying in Europe?
+
Yes. In Italy you can work up to 20 hours/week legally, typically earning ₹700–₹900/hour. Germany also allows 20 hours/week, often more during holidays. Many students cover a significant portion of their living costs through part-time work.
How much bank balance do my parents need to show?
+
Requirements vary by country and visa type. For Italy, a bank statement showing sufficient living funds is required. For Germany, the blocked account (₹9–10L) effectively serves as the financial proof. We guide you through the exact requirements for your specific case during counselling.
When should I start the application process?
+
Ideally 12–18 months before your intended start date. For September intake, start applying October–February. For January intake, apply March–June. IELTS preparation alone typically takes 3–6 months. The earlier you start, the more options you have.
What happens after I graduate — can I stay and work?
+
Absolutely. Germany offers an 18-month job search visa post-graduation. Italy offers a 12-month stay-back for job hunting. Both countries have strong job markets — especially Germany for engineers — and pathways to long-term EU residency.
